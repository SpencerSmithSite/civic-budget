# Research: ERP and budgeting software used by Ohio local governments (2026-09-18)

Compiled by a research agent from vendor sites, customer training guides, Capterra listings, and
press releases. Ohio usage is marked verified only where a named Ohio customer or an official
Ohio source was found.

## Vendors

### 1. UAN, Uniform Accounting Network (Ohio Auditor of State)
- Ohio usage (verified): state-built, free to the entity; the AOS says 2,000+ Ohio townships,
  villages, libraries, special districts and small cities use it (https://uanlink.ohioauditor.gov/,
  version 2026.3). The dominant product for small Ohio entities.
- Screens: training PDFs, e.g. the Accounting Refresher Handbook pp. 94-101
  (https://uanlink.ohioauditor.gov/communications/pdf/Accounting%20Refresher%20Handbook.pdf).
- Visual design: a Windows desktop application. Pale blue-grey form backgrounds, grey group boxes
  with captions ("Account", "Details", "Bank"), required or focused fields highlighted light green,
  Ohio seal in the title bar. Menu-path navigation (Accounting > Utilities > Receipt Utility). Plain
  white grids with bold headers (Fund | Account Code | Account Name | Amount), no striping, Save and
  Close bottom-right. No charts, no dashboard. Reads as Windows XP/7 era; desktop only. Its mental
  model (Fund > Account Code > appropriation/receipt distribution lines) is the Ohio baseline every
  fiscal officer knows.

### 3. Tyler Technologies, Enterprise ERP (Munis) and Tyler Hub
- Ohio usage (verified): Butler County (2012 press release), Clermont County (client logo; 2020
  transparency press release), Union County (the auditor hosts Munis and Tyler Hub guides:
  https://www.unioncountyohio.gov/media/Officials/Auditor/Budgetary%20Resources/Munis%20Docs/Tyler%20Hub%202021.1%20User%20Guide.pdf).
  Larger counties and cities.
- Screens: Union County Tyler Hub guide p. 4; Clayton County GA Munis GL/Budget guide pp. 11 and 30
  (https://techtraining.claytoncountyga.gov/wp-content/uploads/2022/04/Munis-General-Ledger-and-Budget-Overview-End-User-Guide-2018.pdf).
- Visual design: Tyler Hub has a dark navy top bar (about #1F2A78) with global search, a thin green
  sub-bar, a collapsible icon rail on the left, and a card canvas with big solid KPI tiles (teal
  Approvals, orange Notifications, green Alerts) plus tree-menu cards. Munis program screens: navy
  header, a wide ribbon toolbar with labeled icons (Back, Search, Browse, Output, Print, Display,
  PDF, Excel, Word, Email, Schedule, Attach). Account Inquiry uses Fund/Org/Object/Project segment
  pickers and a 4-year comparison matrix (Original Budget, Transfers In/Out, Revised Budget, Actual,
  Encumbrances, Requisitions, Available, Percent used). Flat grey inputs, Roboto-like sans, uppercase
  tab labels with a blue underline. Reads as modernized legacy; form-heavy.

## Cross-cutting conventions (what a finance director expects)
- Fund-first hierarchy everywhere: Fund > Department/Org > Object (> Project). Account strings shown
  as coded numbers with names ("101 - General Fund", "2171-002-0000").
- Budget-vs-actual matrix columns: Original Budget, Revisions/Transfers, Revised Budget,
  Encumbrances, Actual/Expended, Unencumbered Balance, % Used. Multi-year comparison tabs.
- Left module navigation (accordion or tree) with modules named GL, Budgeting, Purchasing, AP,
  Payroll, Receipts.
- Dense grids with column filters, grouping, striping, checkbox selection, and a toolbar of
  Add/Edit/Delete/Export to Excel/Print/PDF.
- Workflow status pills (Approved, In Review, On Hold) and phase steppers for budget cycles.
- Card dashboards with KPI tiles and small charts; pastel categorical palettes for funds and
  departments.
- Export and print are first class (Excel and PDF icons on every screen).
- A blue/navy primary with white surfaces; red/green for variance; institutional seals on
  public-facing pages.

## Design takeaways for CivicBudget
1. Borrow the vocabulary, not the chrome: use the Ohio column set (Appropriation, Encumbered,
   Expended, Unencumbered Balance, % Used where they apply) and UAN-style fund and account coding so
   it reads as native to Ohio fiscal officers.
2. Navy/blue institutional primary, white content, grey canvas; every credible vendor and Ohio
   Checkbook do this. Avoid trendy purples and gradients.
3. Left module nav with a collapsible icon rail (Tyler Hub and Springbrook pattern) rather than
   VIP's loud blue accordion; group by GL, Budget, Purchasing, Reports, Portal.
4. A modern dense data grid (sticky header, column filters, group-by, zebra rows, Excel/PDF export
   in the toolbar, inline edit for budget lines). Density is a credibility signal.
5. Budget workflow stepper plus status pills (OpenGov) for department submission > review >
   council adoption; the most visibly modern feature and it maps directly to Ohio's appropriation
   cycle.
6. KPI tiles plus stacked-bar "broken down by Fund/Department/Object" charts with a muted
   categorical palette; an empty-state treatment for periods without data.
7. The public portal should echo Ohio Checkbook (seal, big-number tiles, "Expand All"
   hierarchical table, filter chips, share/print) but with a responsive layout and accessible
   contrast; the incumbents are mostly desktop-only.
8. Modernize typography and spacing (Inter or Source Sans, 13-14px grid text, 8px rhythm, subtle
   1px borders instead of grey group boxes); that alone distinguishes CivicBudget from UAN and
   Munis while keeping the layout familiar.
